CTF Participation Highlights

### NCL Fall 2026 Individual Game — Top 8.5%

I ranked in the **top 8.5% out of 5,970 competitors** in the National Cyber League (NCL) Fall 2026 Individual Game. Throughout the competition, I applied my knowledge of cybersecurity across several disciplines, including Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT), cryptography, network traffic analysis, digital forensics, and web application security. One of my biggest takeaways was learning how to approach unfamiliar problems, research new technologies, and adapt my methods when my initial approach wasn't effective. I particularly enjoyed the investigative aspects of the competition, where I could piece together seemingly unrelated information to uncover meaningful findings. This experience strengthened my technical problem-solving skills and reinforced my interest in pursuing a career in security operations and digital forensics.

### NCL Fall 2026 Team Game — Top 5%

As the **team captain** during the National Cyber League (NCL) Fall 2026 Team Game, I led a six-member team to a **top 5% finish**. My primary responsibility was coordinating our efforts, delegating tasks, and ensuring we worked efficiently as a team. I developed a delegation strategy that assigned each member three challenge categories based on their individual strengths, designating one as their primary responsibility and two as secondary areas of support. I also established a three-day execution plan that prioritized individual assignments before transitioning to collaborative problem-solving and completing any remaining challenges. Throughout the competition, I monitored our progress, encouraged communication, and helped redirect resources where additional support was needed. This experience strengthened my leadership, organizational, and decision-making skills while demonstrating the importance of effective delegation, teamwork, and adaptability in cybersecurity.

### Skills Learned & Developed

Participating in the National Cyber League (NCL) Fall 2026 competitions allowed me to expand my technical knowledge and develop practical skills across several cybersecurity disciplines. I gained valuable hands-on experience in **Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT), digital forensics, cryptography, network traffic analysis, web application security, and system analysis**. One of the most valuable skills I developed was learning how to approach unfamiliar problems, identify relevant tools, and adapt my investigative methods when initial approaches were unsuccessful. I also strengthened my ability to analyze large amounts of information, recognize patterns, and draw meaningful conclusions from limited evidence. Beyond the technical aspects, serving as a team captain helped me improve my **leadership, communication, delegation, and time management skills**. Most importantly, these competitions taught me that success in cybersecurity isn't always about knowing the answer immediately, but rather having the persistence, curiosity, and resourcefulness to find it.
